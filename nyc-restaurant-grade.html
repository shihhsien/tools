What do these grades mean?

Grades reflect points for health-code violations found at inspection — lower is better. A = 0–13 points, B = 14–27, C = 28 or more.

Critical violations (pests, unsafe food temperatures, bare-hand contact) carry more points than upkeep issues, so one critical finding can move a grade more than several minor ones.

About 9 in 10 NYC restaurants score an A, so a B or C is genuinely worth noting. But an A right at 13 points is borderline — the score matters as much as the letter.

“Grade Pending” usually means a recent below-A inspection awaiting a re-inspection (often within about 30 days) or an open appeal — not a final grade.

“Closed by DOHMH” means a serious hazard (e.g. severe pests, no hot water); the restaurant can’t reopen until it’s fixed and the city re-approves.

Inspections recur on a sliding schedule: roughly once a year for an A, more often (every few months) for a B or C.

Letter grading is credited with real food-safety gains — a CDC study linked it to a measurable drop in Salmonella cases citywide.
